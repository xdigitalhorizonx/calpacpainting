Boards & property managers

A community repaint is a logistics job with painting in it.

The paint is the easy part. What makes or breaks an association repaint is everything around it — which buildings go in which phase, when residents get told, where the crew parks, whether the cars have to move, and who signs off at the end.

Cal Pac has painted the Las Vegas valley since 1991 — communities across Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas and Boulder City. We carry a $750,000 bid limit, so a full-community scope fits inside one contract, and we are small enough to take a single clubhouse or a run of perimeter wall when that is all the reserve study will fund this year.

What a community repaint covers

One contractor for the whole property.

Buildings, iron, curbs and the lot — the whole scope sits on one license, so a board is not coordinating four vendors across one schedule.

Core scope

Community exteriors

Building elevations, stucco, trim, garage doors, eaves and fascia — prepared, primed where needed, and finished to the association's approved color scheme.

Clubhouse & common areas

Interiors for clubhouses, leasing offices, corridors, stairwells, mail rooms and fitness rooms — worked around the hours residents actually use them.

Wrought iron & railings

Gates, fencing, stair rails and balcony railings — the details that show rust first and date a community fastest.

Graffiti removal

Abatement matched back to the original finish — color, sheen and texture, not just coverage.

Striping, curbs & stenciling

Parking re-striping, ADA stalls, red curb and stencil work, sequenced so residents always have somewhere to park.

REO painting & compliance

Bank-owned and compliance work, brought back to a standard the association can accept — a scope Cal Pac has handled for years.

How a Cal Pac community repaint runs

From first walk to final inspection — and who stands behind it.

1 · Walk the property

We walk the community with the board or manager and scope it building by building — substrate condition, prep required, and what genuinely needs doing this cycle versus next.

2 · A bid you can read

The proposal states the scope, the preparation included, the products specified and the phasing — the level of detail a board needs to approve it in a meeting and a manager needs to defend it afterwards.

3 · Phase and notify

The community is split into work zones with dates attached, so notices go out with real information on them and residents know when their building and their parking are affected.

4 · Inspect and hand over

A multi-point quality inspection on completion, a walk with the board, and a one-year workmanship warranty with a direct customer-service line behind it.

Board questions

Before you put us on the agenda.

How large a community can Cal Pac take on?

Cal Pac carries a $750,000 commercial bid limit, which covers most full-community repaints in the Las Vegas valley. We are also set up for smaller scopes — a single building, a clubhouse, a run of perimeter wall — so a board does not have to bundle work it is not ready to fund.

How do you work around residents during a repaint?

The community gets split into work zones with dates attached, so board notices go out with real information on them. A painting supervisor keeps the job-site record as the work happens, and the crew leaves each zone picked up at the end of the day.

Are you licensed and bonded in Nevada?

Yes. Cal Pac Painting of Nevada, Inc. holds Nevada contractor license #0044823. The company was founded in California in 1971, expanded into the Las Vegas valley in 1991, and incorporated in Nevada in 1996.

What warranty comes with the work?

Every client receives a one-year workmanship warranty and a direct customer-service telephone line. Every job also gets a multi-point quality inspection on completion.

Do you handle specification and compliance requirements?

Yes. Meeting government, builder and association specifications on each project is a priority for us, and we use only approved materials, supplies and equipment. Cal Pac also does REO painting and compliance work.

How often do Las Vegas communities need a repaint?

There is no single number — desert sun, stucco condition, the quality of the last paint job and the association's reserve schedule all move it. What we can do is walk the property, tell you honestly which elevations still have life in them, and scope only what actually needs doing this cycle.

What paperwork can you provide for our management packet?

License verification for Nevada contractor #0044823, insurance certificates on request, and a written scope that states the preparation and products included — the documentation a community manager needs before the board votes.

Do your crews speak Spanish?

Yes — se habla español, in the office and on the job site.
