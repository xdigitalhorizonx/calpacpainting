Choosing exterior colors that survive the desert sun.

The Mojave is one of the hardest paint environments in the country. Color choice is not just taste here — it decides how soon you repaint.

By Cal Pac Painting of Nevada · August 22, 2026

Why the desert eats some colors alive

Every paint color is a recipe of pigments, and pigments age differently under UV. The Mojave's sun load breaks down organic pigments — the bright, saturated reds, some blues, deep vivid tones — far faster than inorganic, earth-based ones. That is why a vivid burgundy door can soften toward pink in a couple of summers while the tan wall around it barely moves.

Heat is the second force. Dark colors absorb it: a deep charcoal wall in July runs dramatically hotter than a pale one, and that thermal cycling stresses both the paint film and the stucco behind it. It is not that you cannot paint dark in Las Vegas — it is that dark asks more of the product, the prep and the wall.

The colors that hold

  • Light and mid-tone neutrals — sands, warm grays, soft whites — reflect heat and hide fade longest. There is a reason the valley's housing stock lives in this range.
  • Earth-pigment tones — terracottas, ochres, muted browns — age gracefully because their pigments are the same minerals the desert itself is made of.
  • Soft blues and blue-grays hold better than saturated primaries and read beautifully against desert light — pale tones especially.
  • Whites and off-whites for trim stay crisp and make the field color look intentional. Most of the sharpness you notice on a fresh repaint is really the trim line.

Working inside an HOA scheme

Most Las Vegas communities paint from an approved palette, and the schemes exist for a reason — they are usually already desert-tested. The room to move is in the combinations: field, trim, accent and door. If your association is due for a repaint, the time to raise a scheme change is before the bid, not after the first building is done. (Boards weighing a full-community job may find our nine questions for hiring a painting contractor useful.)

Test it on the wall, not in your hand

Paint chips lie. They are printed ink viewed indoors at reading distance; your wall is textured stucco under the brightest light in North America. Before committing a whole exterior:

  • Paint a real sample patch — a few square feet — on the actual wall.
  • Put one patch on the sunniest elevation and one in shade; the same color reads differently on each.
  • Look at it at morning, noon and dusk. Desert light swings warm–harsh–warm through the day, and colors swing with it.
  • Step across the street. Exterior color is read at fifty feet, not five.

Rule of thumb: in this valley, whatever you pick will read one to two shades lighter and warmer outside than it did indoors. Choose slightly deeper than your first instinct.
